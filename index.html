The pressure points

What makes growth hard?

The challenges rarely arrive one at a time. Costly credit, unreliable services and thin business systems can reinforce one another.

01

Finance that is hard to reach

Smaller firms are less likely to use bank loans. Collateral requirements, interest costs and limited financial records can make expansion funding difficult to secure. 2 3

02

Unreliable, costly operations

Power outages, utility tariffs and road or border delays disrupt production and add working-capital pressure—especially for firms using imported inputs. 2 4

03

Informality and admin friction

Most establishments are informal. Registration, recordkeeping, tax compliance and permits can consume scarce owner time and complicate access to formal markets. 1 5

04

Skills and digital adoption gaps

Many firms have not yet upgraded marketing, online sales or performance systems. New tools need complementary skills, reliable power and time to implement. 6

05

Markets are not equally open

Trade compliance, import delays, limited export support and uneven digital reach can make it difficult for small firms to find customers and scale beyond local markets. 4 6

06

Volatility makes planning harder

Inflation, exchange-rate swings and changing input costs can unsettle pricing, inventory and cash flow. Conditions have improved in some measures, but shocks still matter. 7

The survey signal

Finance leads the list.

When registered firms with five or more employees were asked to name their single biggest business-environment obstacle, access to finance came first. Tax rates were next. This is a snapshot of the formal, non-agricultural firms covered by the survey—not every Ghanaian microbusiness.

Different lenses, different firms

Scale changes the picture.

The takeaway

The challenge is not just starting a business. It is building one that can withstand shocks and grow.

Limited credit, small scale, informality, infrastructure costs and capability gaps can compound one another. The most reliable picture comes from combining broad GSS census data with carefully qualified firm-level surveys.
